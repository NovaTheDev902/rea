# BIGPRETZEL — Android Forensic Indicator of Compromise

## Summary (Confidence: HIGH — verified 2-1 and 3-0 across merged claims)
**BIGPRETZEL** is a forensic artifact uniquely associated with Paragon Graphite spyware on Android devices. It was identified on the devices of Italian activists and independently confirmed by WhatsApp as a reliable indicator of Graphite compromise.

## What It Is
BIGPRETZEL appears to be an artifact (log entry, file, or process trace) left in the Android system by Graphite's cross-app propagation mechanism. The exact technical form (filename, log key, process name) is not publicly disclosed — Citizen Lab refers to it by this codename.

## Confirmed Detections
| Victim | Dates of BIGPRETZEL Artifacts | Confirmed By |
|--------|-------------------------------|--------------|
| Giuseppe Caccia (Italian activist) | Dec 22, 2024 – Jan 31, 2025 (multiple dates) | Citizen Lab forensic analysis |
| Luca Casarini (Italian activist) | Dec 23, 2024 | Citizen Lab forensic analysis |

Both Caccia and Casarini were confirmed as targets of Italian government-operated Graphite by Italy's COPASIR parliamentary oversight committee and Italian prosecutors.

## WhatsApp's Independent Confirmation
WhatsApp independently confirmed that BIGPRETZEL is **uniquely associated with Paragon Graphite** — meaning it is not a false positive generated by other software. This makes BIGPRETZEL a high-confidence indicator for forensic triage.

## How It's Used in Forensics
Researchers looking for Graphite infections on Android devices look for BIGPRETZEL in:
- System logs
- App-specific directories
- Process traces

Detection tools: **MVT (Mobile Verification Toolkit)** by Amnesty International Security Lab can scan for this and other Graphite-related IOCs when provided with updated STIX indicator files.

## Note: SMALLPRETZEL Claim Refuted
Some sources claimed there is an iOS counterpart called **SMALLPRETZEL** (anomalous CloudKit activity). This claim was refuted 0-3 by adversarial verification — SMALLPRETZEL is **not confirmed** in primary Citizen Lab reporting. On iOS, the forensic detection method is **Fingerprint P1** (see that folder), not SMALLPRETZEL.
